# Scope change, 2026-11-11

USCIS forms are filed on the USCIS online portal. This tool therefore does **data retrieval only**:
documents in, verified values out, a person types them into the portal. It does not automate the
portal (accounts, CAPTCHAs, terms of use).

## What is built
- Readers: passport and visa (machine-readable lines with check digits), I-94 printout (printed
  labels, no checksum). OCR is local Tesseract; nothing leaves the machine.
- Passport-first reconciliation: passport > visa > I-94. A lower-ranked document that disagrees is
  flagged ("Documents differ") and never silently resolved. Failed check digits and same-rank
  disagreements block the data sheet until the preparer fixes them.
- Data sheet (text / CSV / JSON) with dates in MM/DD/YYYY, the source of each value, what needs
  attention, what is still empty, and the SHA-256 of every input file.

## Parked (kept in the repo, not used by the window)
- `filler.py`, `forms/`, `forms/i-765.pdf`: fills the blank I-765 PDF while keeping the XFA layer.
  Acrobat behaviour of its output was never confirmed. See `docs/spike-findings.md`.

## Scan reading (updated 2026-11-12)
Tested on two public-domain specimen data pages (`tests/fixtures/specimens/SOURCES.md`) made
awkward the way real scans are: small on a Letter page, rotated 90/180/270, skewed, blurred,
low resolution, and PDFs carrying an unrelated invisible text layer.
- Before: 9 of 48 test scans read. After: 41 of 48. All 24 Norwegian-specimen variants read.
  The 7 that still fail are the 669 px Taiwan specimen shrunk or skewed until the letters are
  about 10 px tall; Tesseract cannot read those.
- Cause of the original failure: the reader only looked at clean, full-frame, upright images, and
  skipped OCR whenever a PDF already had any text layer.
- What check digits do NOT cover: names, sex, nationality and issuing country. Only document
  number, date of birth and expiry date are protected, and mod-10 misses some substitutions (the
  document number read `CCCOO2251` for `CCC002251` and still passed). So the reader also compares
  names and the passport number with the printed page (exact match; transliteration digraphs such
  as OE/AA tolerated) and flags names that look misread. In the audit, 7 of 8 wrong values were
  flagged and 1 (a heavily degraded image) was not. Roughly a third of correct readings on
  degraded images are flagged anyway, because the page OCR is also imperfect; the preparer then
  types the name.
- Position-based corrections: a digit in a country code or name is always corrected to a letter
  (`N0R` to `NOR`); a letter in a date or check-digit position is corrected only if every check
  digit then passes. Trailing `<` padding misread as K or digits is rebuilt; names are never
  altered by it (a bug that ate "ESSE" from JESSE was found by a unit test and fixed).

## Not verified
- The I-94 reader was written from the labels on the CBP website and tested on synthetic text.
  It needs a real I-94 printout (redacted is fine) before it can be trusted.
- No real passport has been read: the user's own files were deliberately never opened.
- The native window's file dialogs, drag-and-drop and Copy button have not been clicked through.

## Not read yet
Passport visual zone (place of birth is not in the MRZ), I-20, DS-2019, EAD, driver's license,
birth and marriage certificates, tax and employment documents.
